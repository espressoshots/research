Research

What the work has shown so far

A TADF emitter is only as good as its slowest step. Triplets have to climb back to the singlet state before they are lost, so the molecule needs a small singlet–triplet gap (ΔEST), enough spin–orbit coupling to make the crossing fast, and few non-radiative ways out. I work on cyanopyridine and benzoylpyridine acceptors and use synthesis, photophysics and quantum chemistry together, so that each structural change can be traced to a rate constant and then to a device number.

Molecular design · headline result

Symmetric coupling rescues a weak emitter

Joining two units of a poorly performing cyanopyridine emitter (tCzPyCN) into a symmetric dual-core molecule (2tCz2Py2CN) narrowed ΔEST from 0.21 to 0.08 eV. EQEmax rose from 0.94 % to 15.02 % and PLQY from 33.4 % to 59.1 %, because the competing non-radiative triplet decay is suppressed (knrT = 0.11 × 105 s−1).

The point is general: an emitter that fails on its own can be recovered by changing how its units are connected, without redesigning the chromophore.

Chem. Commun. 2026 · first author

Structure–property

Tuning the acceptor core of cyanopyridines

How tuning the acceptor core within a single cyanopyridine donor–acceptor framework leads to efficient TADF emitters: the acceptor, not only the donor, as the design variable.

Adding a second, asymmetric pyridine acceptor (2tCzPyCN → 2tCz2PyCN) shifts emission from deep blue to sky blue and lowers the S1/S0 reorganisation energy from 0.94 to 0.82 eV, which suppresses non-radiative decay roughly fifteen-fold.

Chem. Asian J. 2026 · first author

Solid state

Aggregation as a handle on spin-flipping

In two benzoylpyridine emitters that differ in donor size (BPy-pTC and BPy-p3C), only the bulkier one shows aggregation-induced enhanced emission, and its aggregates reach kRISC of 2.06 × 105 s−1. Solid films also switch their emission reversibly under acid and base vapour.

Aggregation narrows ΔEST in both, but in BPy-p3C it inverts the gap, placing T1 0.047 eV above S1.

Beilstein J. Org. Chem. 2022 · first author

Theory

Excited-state calculations alongside the bench

DFT and TD-DFT for design and state assignment; SCS-CC2 and SCS-ADC(2) benchmarks where TD-DFT overestimates ΔEST in multiple-resonance systems; spin–orbit and vibronic coupling, reorganisation energies and Marcus-type rate analysis for reverse intersystem crossing.
